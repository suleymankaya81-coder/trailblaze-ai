## Getting there and getting around

- [Cooktown parks map(PDF, 288KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/153806/cooktown-parks-map.pdf)

Kalkajaka National Park (CYPAL) is on Cape York Peninsula. The car park and lookout are 25km south-west of Cooktown on the Mulligan Highway.

When approaching from the south, travel along the Peninsula Developmental Road to Lakeland Downs. From here turn onto the Mulligan Highway and head north-east towards Cooktown. These roads are sealed and are suitable for conventional vehicles for most of the year. Heavy rain during the wet season may result in localised flooding, closing the roads for short periods of time.

Approaching from the north, the Peninsula Developmental Road to Lakeland Downs is mostly unsealed. Always drive according to prevailing conditions. Four-wheel-drive vehicles are recommended for this route.

May to October is the best time to travel on Cape York Peninsula. Many roads and tracks become impassable during the hotter, wet season months from late November to April.

Contact the [Department of Transport and Main Roads](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) for local road conditions and the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/) for updated weather reports.

### Wheelchair accessibility

The viewpoint at the car park is wheelchair accessible.

## Staying safe

- Do not risk injury by venturing onto the boulder field—people have been injured and have died trying to climb Kalkajaka.
- Wear a hat, sunglasses, long-sleeved shirt and sunscreen, even on cloudy days, to avoid sunburn.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

- binoculars for better wildlife viewing
- a hat, sunglasses and sunscreen
- drinking water
- rubbish bags—bins are not provided.

### Opening hours

Kalkajaka National Park (CYPAL) is open 24 hours a day, seven days a week, weather permitting. Many roads and tracks become impassable during the hotter, wet season months from late November to April.

### Permits and fees

Permits are required for all [commercial](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/hospitality-tourism-sport/tourism/parks-recreation-forests) or [organised events](https://apps.des.qld.gov.au/forms/organised-events/). View [permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted in Kalkajaka National Park (CYPAL).

### Climate and weather

Cape York's seasons are divided into 'the wet' and 'the dry'. During the wet, the area can be deluged by heavy monsoonal storms, rain depressions and cyclones. Winter temperatures can drop below 10°C and summer temperatures can soar beyond 40°C. Typical temperatures are however, 18–26°C in winter and 23–32°C in summer, usually with high humidity.

May to October is the best time to travel on Cape York Peninsula. Many roads and tracks become impassable during the hotter, wet season months from late November to April. Contact [Department of Transport and Main Roads](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) for local road conditions. Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Cooktown. For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/kalkajaka/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
