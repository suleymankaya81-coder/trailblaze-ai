## Getting there and getting around

![On the beach at Cedar Bay. Photo: Queensland Government.](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/image/0015/152700/varieties/thumb.jpg)

**Map:** [Mangkalba (Cedar Bay) section map, Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL)(PDF, 215KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/156236/mangkalba_map.pdf)

Mangkalba (Cedar Bay) section of Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL) is 40km south of Cooktown or 10km north of Ayton. Lying between Cape Tribulation and Cooktown, Cedar Bay is accessible only by boat or on foot via the Home Rule track.

Check [park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) and [Queensland Traffic](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) to enquire about local road conditions. The [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/) provides updated weather reports.

**Access to [Marbaymba (Rattlesnake Point)(PDF, 96KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/157392/marbaymba-rattlesnake-point-raa.pdf) is prohibited to protect significant cultural resources.**

#### Boat access

Boat access is difficult in most conditions, especially during prevailing south-east winds above 15 knots. A fringing reef along much of the bay prevents easy access by boat. The best entrance points are at the far northern and more southern ends of the bay where sand allows for safe, easy access. Hope Islands, the closest sheltered anchorage, is about 10km away and public moorings are available.

#### Walking track access

The Home Rule track traverses the Mangkalba (Cedar Bay) section of Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL) providing access into Cedar Bay. The track begins at Home Rule Rainforest Lodge, a private property 3km from Rossville.

### Wheelchair accessibility

There are no wheelchair-accessible facilities or tracks in Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL).

## Staying safe

![Image of a dangerous stinging box jellyfish (stinger) which swimmers need to be aware of.](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/image/0026/157193/varieties/thumb.jpg)

Be aware of dangerous stinging box jellyfish (stingers)

Photo credit: Jamie Seymour © James Cook University

You are responsible for your own safety. Follow the guidelines below for a safe and enjoyable visit.

- Be aware that crocodiles can turn up anywhere in croc country, including tidal reaches of rivers, along beaches, on offshore islands and cays in the Great Barrier Reef and Torres Strait, and in freshwater lagoons, rivers and swamps. Crocodiles are dangerous and attacks can be fatal. Remember to be [Be Crocwise](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/becrocwise) in croc country!
- Dangerous stinging jellyfish (stingers) may be present in the coastal waters at any time, but occur more frequently in the warmer months. Visit [Beachsafe(external link)](https://beachsafe.org.au/surf-safety/tropical-stingers) for the latest safety and first-aid information.
- Take care around cassowaries . Please slow down when driving through their habitat and watch out for cassowaries and their chicks at the roadside. These large birds can cause serious injury or death. Stay well away and never feed cassowaries. Be cassoWARY.
- Be alert for snakes and take care to avoid disturbing them. Keep your distance if you do encounter a snake.
- Inform a reliable person of your itinerary including starting and finishing times. Make sure to tell them when you return to avoid an unnecessary search.
- Plan to complete your walk well before dark.
- Never walk alone. Groups of 4 are recommended.
- Carry at least 1 form of communication equipment. Satellite phones and [personal locator beacons(external link)](https://beacons.amsa.gov.au/about/) (PLBs) are the most effective. Mobile phone coverage is unreliable.
- Stay on the track at all times and follow the trail markers.
- Creek beds and rock surfaces can be slippery. Care is required when traversing these surfaces.
- Always carry adequate drinking water. Remember to drink regularly to avoid heat stress.
- Wear a hat, shirt and sunscreen, even on overcast days, to avoid sunburn.
- Wear protective clothing (long sleeves and long pants) to protect yourself from lawyer vine, leeches, mosquitoes and sandflies.
- Always carry a first-aid kit, map and compass, and know how to use them.
- Pack lightly. Do not carry glass or other heavy, bulky food items.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

For a safe and enjoyable visit remember to bring:

- lightweight food and drinking water
- a fuel stove and fuel
- rubbish bags
- insect repellent and a mosquito net
- first-aid kit
- map and compass.

### Opening hours

Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL) is open 24 hours a day.

**Access to [Marbaymba (Rattlesnake Point)(PDF, 96KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/157392/marbaymba-rattlesnake-point-raa.pdf) is prohibited to protect significant cultural resources.**

### Permits and fees

#### Camping permits

Camping permits are required and must be booked in advance. [Fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply. Your booking number must be displayed at your camp site.

- Find out more about [camping at Mangkalba (Cedar Bay), Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/ngalba-bulal/camping).

#### Other permits

Permits are required for commercial and some organised group activities. See [park permits and policies](https://parks.qld.gov.au/management/permits) for more information.

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted in Ngalba-bulal National Park.

### Climate and weather

Ngalba-bulal National Park (CYPAL) has a tropical climate. In summer the temperatures and humidity are high. From April to September the days are cooler and less humid. Visiting in the cooler winter months is recommended. Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Ayton, Rossville, Helenvale and Cooktown.

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
