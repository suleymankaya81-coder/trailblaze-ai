## Getting there and getting around

- [Parks of the Cooper Creek catchment—park maps(PDF, 1.4MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0018/161442/cooper-creek-catchment-map.pdf)
- [Parks of Central West Queensland map(PDF, 1.2MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0025/163834/centralwestparks-map.pdf)

Forest Den National Park is part of the Cooper Creek catchment. To help you plan your visit to this remote area, simply download the [Central West Queensland National Parks visitor guide(PDF, 4.8MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/151880/central-west-qld-guide.pdf).

Forest Den National Park is 100km north of Aramac, via Torrens Creek Road. Turn east at the ‘Corinda’ signpost and travel 5km before turning left and heading north for 4.5km to Four Mile Waterhole camping area.

A 4WD vehicle is recommended. Even a small amount of rain can make the roads impassable, always be prepared and have at least 2 weeks’ worth of extra supplies in case of stranding. Remember to:

- Check with [Queensland Traffic](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) or [local council offices](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/forest-den/about#tourism_information_links) for current road conditions.
- Check the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/) for weather information.
- Check [Park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Forest Den National Park for the latest information on access, closures and conditions.

### Wheelchair accessibility

There are no wheelchair-accessible facilities at Forest Den National Park.

## Staying safe

**This park is remote and rangers may not be on park to help you.**

**Isolation, hot weather, unexpected rain and the potential vehicle breakdowns require that you plan well ahead for your visit—you must be totally self-sufficient and prepared for all emergencies.**

Be sure to read [essentials to bring](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/forest-den/visiting-safely#before_you_visit) and follow the guidelines below to help make your visit a safe and enjoyable one.

### Before you leave

- **Thoroughly check the condition of your vehicle** and pack vehicle spares, recovery and repair equipment. Include 2 spare tyres, engine coolant and oil. Read [4WD with care](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/four-wheel-drive-with-care) for important information on 4WD safety and minimal impact driving.
- **Choose your destination wisely!** Make sure you are familiar with your equipment, vehicle and someone in your group has experience with remote area travel and inland Australian conditions. For more remote parks, ensure one person has sound mechanical knowledge of your vehicle.
- **Expect rough, slow, dusty and/or boggy roads**, far from help if something should go wrong. Check local road and weather conditions and extended weather forecasts for the locations you intend to visit.
- **Plan to fuel up regularly**. Calculate driving times and distances accurately and plan your refuelling points. Driving time on an unsealed road is much longer than on a bitumen road! Frequent low gear and 4WD travel will use more fuel too. Remember, national park bases and local properties are not service stations.
- **Leave an itinerary** with a friend or relative. Include travel routes and/or check-in points.
- **Plan for emergencies**. Pack reliable communication equipment, extra supplies and a list of local UHF radio channels and emergency contacts.
- [Check current weather warning for Queensland(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/). Flood waters can flow down watercourses from hundreds of miles away, even if there has been no local rain.
- **Pack for hot and cold conditions** (plus flies and midges!). Outback Queensland can be very hot during the day, and very cold (to below freezing point) overnight. Check with the [Australian Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/) for more information on average temperatures.
- **Seek local advice.** Contact relevant [tourist information centres](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/forest-den/about#tourism_infromation_links) to help plan your trip.
- **Book your camp site.** To camp in a national park, you must plan well ahead and obtain a camping permit and pay your camping fee before you arrive. Remember, mobile phone coverage is very limited in Central West Queensland national parks. Visit [Qld camping](https://parks.desi.qld.gov.au/camping) for detailed information on camping bookings and fees.

### While visiting

- **Keep to designated roads and tracks**, and drive with caution as road conditions can change quickly. Watch out for animals, they can appear on roads at any time of day though are generally more active at dusk and dawn.
- **Stay with your vehicle if it breaks down and wait for help to arrive**. A vehicle is much easier to find than a person.
- **Always carry drinking water**, whether driving or walking. Boil, filter or treat water from streams, rivers or waterholes before drinking.
- **Never jump or dive into water**. It may be shallow or hide submerged objects.
- **Wear protective clothing**. Put on a hat, sunscreen, insect repellent and wear sturdy footwear, not thongs.
- **Be aware of your surroundings** at all times and be on the lookout for animals and insects that could scratch, sting or bite.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

### In an emergency

**For all emergencies call Triple Zero (000)**.

We highly recommend you visit the [Triple Zero website(external link)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/) before visiting a national park or forest. Download the [Triple Zero emergency app(external link)](https://www.emergencyplus.com.au/) to help identify your location. **Important:** if there is no mobile coverage on any network, you will not be able to reach the Emergency Call Service via a mobile phone.

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

- Sufficient water, food and emergency supplies. Carry at least 7 litres of water per person per day and enough emergency food and water for at least 2 weeks in case of stranding.
- A portable fuel stove (recommended) and/or clean firewood such as untreated, mill off-cuts.
- Reliable camping gear in good working order.
- Sturdy rubbish bags and sealable animal-proof containers.
- Complete first-aid kit and medical supplies. Include sun and insect protection in your kit.
- Communication equipment and a list of emergency contacts. A UHF, satellite phone and/or a personal locator beacon (PLB).
- Detailed road maps showing travel routes. A GPS may be useful.
- Extra fuel, vehicle repair equipment and spares. Frequent low gear and four-wheel-drive travel will use fuel more quickly on park drives. You should also bring additional spare tyres, oil and engine coolant and vehicle recovery gear.

### Opening hours

Forest Den National Park is open all year round. The park may be closed at times due to management operations or severe weather. Check [park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Forest Den Nation Park for the latest information on national park access, closures and conditions.

### Permits and fees

Camping permits are required and [fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply.

- Book your camp site [online](https://www.qld.gov.au/camping).
- If you cannot book online, see [camping bookings](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) for other options.

#### Other permits and fees

Various activities in Queensland parks and forests require a permit. Activities include commercial tours, social events, organised group visits, school excursions, scientific research, professional photography and the sale of photographs or vision of the park. [Contact us](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted in Forest Den National Park.

### Climate and weather

Visiting is recommended from April to September as summer temperatures reach over 40°C during the day, and summer rains often cause flooding. Rain can fall at any time of year and flooding can occur up to two weeks after rain has fallen elsewhere in the catchment, resulting in unexpected creek rises and road closures.

Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

The nearest fuel and supplies are at Aramac (100km).

- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.
