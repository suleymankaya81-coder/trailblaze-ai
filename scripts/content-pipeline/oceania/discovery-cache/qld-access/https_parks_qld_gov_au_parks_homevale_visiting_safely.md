## Getting there and getting around

- [Mackay Highlands map(PDF, 594KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/167324/mackay-highlands-map.pdf)

Homevale National Park is 70km south-west of Mackay by road, near Nebo.

**From Nebo:** drive back towards Mackay along the Peak Downs Highway for 6km before turning left onto Nebo-Glenden Road. Follow this road for 11.2km and turn right into Turrawulla Road. From this intersection, drive about 23km, passing the Homevale-Mount Britton turnoff, to the signed right-hand turn into Moonlight Dam camping area. From this turnoff, it is 6km to the camping area. Roads within the national park are gravel vehicle tracks, a 4WD and high clearance vehicle is recommended.

It is also possible to reach Homevale from the north via Eungella National Park.

**From Eungella township:** follow the road sweeping left through Eungella National Park to Broken River. From Broken River most of the roads to Homevale are gravel, a 4WD and high clearance vehicle is recommended. From Broken River, follow Eungella Dam Road for 24.5km before turning left into Pipeline Road (also known as Lizzie Creek Road). Follow Pipeline Road for 17.3km and turn left into Turrawulla Road. From this intersection, it is 36.1km to the signed left-hand turn into Moonlight Dam camping area. From this turnoff, it is 6km to the camping area.

### Wheelchair accessibility

There are no wheelchair-accessible facilities in Homevale National Park.

## Staying safe

Consider your fitness, ability and weather conditions carefully before setting out. Be well prepared and responsible for your own safety—even on a short stroll. Do not expect to be warned of every possible danger.

- Never walk alone—walk with family or friends.
- Tell friends or family where you are going and when you expect to return. If you change your plans, inform them. Plan to complete your walk well before dark.
- Keep to tracks to avoid getting lost.
- Carry extra food and drinking water in case your walk takes longer than expected.
- Carry a first-aid kit and know how to use it.
- Wear boots or sturdy shoes for a comfortable walk.
- Dress for comfort, use insect repellent and protect yourself from sun and rain.
- Carry at least one form of communication equipment. Satellite phones and [Personal Locator Beacons(external link)](https://beacons.amsa.gov.au/) (PLBs) are the most effective in this area. Mobile phone coverage is unreliable.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

## In an emergency

### For all emergencies call Triple Zero (000).

We recommend you visit the [Triple Zero website(external link)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/) before visiting the national park. You can also download the free [emergency + app(external link)](https://www.emergencyplus.com.au/) before you leave home, the GPS functionality can provide critical location details to emergency services. **Important:** if there is no mobile coverage on any network, you will not be able to reach the Emergency Call Service via a mobile phone.

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

- sufficient food and water
- first-aid kit—and know how to use it
- fuel stove and fuel—fires are not permitted
- sturdy footwear
- protective clothing
- insect repellent
- ground sheet
- strong rubbish bags—bins are not provided.

### Opening hours

Homevale National Park is open all year around. The park may be closed at times during severe weather, high fire danger or for management activities.

Before visiting the national park, check [park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for up-to-date information on access, closures and conditions in parks and forests. Contact the [Department of Transport and Main Roads](https://www.tmr.qld.gov.au/Travel-and-transport) to find out about local road conditions and the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/) for weather reports and forecasts.

### Permits and fees

Camping permits are required and [fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply.

- Find out more about camping in [Homevale National Park](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/homevale/camping).
- [Book your camp site online](https://www.qld.gov.au/camping).
- If you cannot book online, see [camping bookings](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) for other options.

### Other permits

Various activities in national parks require a permit. Activities include commercial tours, social events (weddings and parties), organised group visits, school excursions, scientific research, and the sale of photographs or vision of the national park. View [permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Dogs and other domestic animals are not permitted in Homevale National Park.

### Climate and weather

The region's dry season occurs during winter (June to August), with average temperatures from 10°C to 20°C. Between October and March, high humidity, strong seasonal rainfall and average temperatures of 20°C to 30°C make walking less comfortable.

Much of the yearly rainfall falls between December and March with the driest months being generally August/September.

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Mackay, Proserpine, Nebo and Eungella. For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/homevale/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
