## Getting there and getting around

- [Blackdown Tableland National Park map(PDF, 1.9MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/163308/blackdown-map.pdf)
- [Munall camping area map(PDF, 1.2MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/163712/munall-camp-map.pdf)

Blackdown Tableland National Park lies in central Queensland 183km west of Rockhampton. The signed turn-off to the national park is on the Capricorn Highway 11km west of Dingo or 35km east of Blackwater.

From the turn-off, follow Charlevue Road for 16km to the base of the tableland. The 6km drive from the base of the tableland to Yaddamen Dhina is narrow, winding and steep. The road is not suitable for towing caravans or heavy trailers.

All roads past Yaddamen Dhina are unsealed (gravel), narrow and winding. Many locations are accessible with a 2WD vehicle driven with caution. A 4WD vehicle is recommended for the road beyond Munall camping area. The 4WD Loop Road to Mitha Boongulla is rough and difficult, and is suitable for high clearance 4WD vehicles only.

Before you arrive, remember to:

- Check [Queensland Traffic](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) or the [local council office](https://beprepared.chrc.qld.gov.au/) for current road conditions to access the national park.
- Check [Park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Blackdown Tableland National Park for the latest information on access, closures and conditions.
- Check **[**Bureau of Meteorology**(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/)** for current weather and warnings.

### Wheelchair accessibility

Wheelchair-accessible toilets are available at Yaddamen Dhina and Munall camping area. The walking track from the car park at Yaddamen Dhina to the lookout is wheelchair-accessible (assistance required).

## Staying safe

Be well prepared and responsible for your own safety—even on a short stroll.

The national park is semi-remote, and rangers may not be available to help you. You must be self-sufficient and well prepared for all emergencies and responsible for your own safety—even on a short stroll.

### **When walking**

- Mind your step! Be aware that pea gravel (that occurs naturally) on the walking tracks makes the ground slippery under foot.

![Take care on the walking tracks. Heed all safety advice and be mindful of pea gravel, it’s slippery!](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/image/0020/425045/varieties/thumb.jpg)

Take care on the walking tracks. Heed all safety advice and be mindful of pea gravel, it’s slippery!

- Never walk alone. Always let a responsible person know where you are going and when you expect to return. Plan to finish your walk in the daylight.
- Keep to the designated marked walking tracks, stay behind safety barriers, heed all safety, and access signage.
- Walk with care. Cliff edges are sheer and may be unstable—keep well back from the edge. Supervise children closely. Significant injury or death can result from a slip or fall.
- Take care at creek crossings, rocks may be uneven and slippery.
- Never dive or jump into creeks. Creeks and waterholes hide dangers such as submerged logs and rocks; water depth is variable and unpredictable.
- Wear protective clothing and sturdy enclosed footwear, a hat and sunscreen. Avoid exercise in the heat of the day.
- Remember to pack drinking water, extra food, a first-aid kit and at least one form of communication equipment.

### **When driving**

- Queensland road rules apply on the national park roads.
- Drive slowly to avoid locking your brakes and losing control.
- Always be alert for other vehicles, especially when approaching blind corners.
- Ensure that your vehicle is in sound mechanical order. Drive according to the road conditions, plus you and your vehicle’s capabilities.

### **In an emergency**

**For all emergencies, call Triple Zero (000)**.

There is variable mobile phone coverage at Yaddamen Dhina lookout only. Satellite phones and [Personal Locator Beacons(external link)](https://beacons.amsa.gov.au/about/beacon-types.asp) (PLBs) are the most effective in the national park.

We recommend you visit the [Triple Zero website(external link)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/) before visiting the national park.

You can also download the free [emergency+ app(external link)](https://www.emergencyplus.com.au/) before you leave home, the GPS functionality can provide critical location details to emergency services. **Important:** if there is no mobile coverage on any network, you will not be able to reach the Emergency Call Service via a mobile phone.

Please read the [stay safe and visit with care](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care) information.

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

Prepare well and remember to bring:

- Enough food and water—there is no drinking water provided.
- A first-aid kit—and know how to use it.
- Reliable communication equipment.
- Sturdy enclosed footwear.
- Sun protection—wear a hat, shirt and sunscreen, even on overcast days, to avoid sunburn.
- Secure containers to store food and rubbish so that it is inaccessible to wildlife—rubbish bins are not provided.
- Firewood—bring only clean milled timber for use as firewood. Collecting fire wood and kindling in the national park is prohibited.
- Enough fuel for your visit.

### Opening hours

Blackdown Tableland National Park is open 24 hours a day. Areas of the national park may be closed at times due to severe weather or park management operations. Check [Park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Blackdown Tableland National Park for the latest information on national park access, closures and conditions.

### Permits and fees

#### Camping

If you intend to camp you will need a camping permit. [Fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply. A camping tag or booking with your details must be displayed at your camp site.

- [Book your campsite online](https://www.qld.gov.au/camping).
- If you cannot book online, see [camping bookings](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) for other options.

#### Other permits

Various activities conducted in the national park may require a permit. These activities include commercial tours, geocaching, social events (such as weddings), organised group visits, school excursions, scientific research, and sale of photographs or vision of Blackdown Tableland National Park. See, [Permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Dogs and other domestic animals are not permitted in Blackdown Tableland National Park.

### Climate and weather

Daytime temperatures average 24-30°C in summer and 5-15°C in winter. Be prepared for cool nights, particularly in winter when temperatures can fall below 0°C. Dense fog often blankets the tableland. Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Dingo, approximately 29km from the national park boundary.

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
