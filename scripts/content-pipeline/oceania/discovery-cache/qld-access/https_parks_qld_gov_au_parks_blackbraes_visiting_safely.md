### Map

- [Blackbraes National Park map(PDF, 1.9MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0030/157584/blackbraes-map.pdf)

Blackbraes National Park is on the Kennedy Developmental Road and can be accessed via Hughenden or the Lynd Junction.

### Hughenden access

From Townsville follow the Flinders Highway 380km south-west, or from Mount Isa, follow the Flinders Highway 520km east. At Hughenden, turn onto the Kennedy Developmental Road and travel north for 170km to the national park.

### Lynd Junction access

Access to the Lynd Junction is from Townsville (via Hervey Range), Charters Towers or Mount Garnet.

- From Townsville, follow Hervey Range Road for 109km through Hervey Range. Turn right onto the Gregory Developmental Road and travel 163km to the Lynd Junction.
- From Charters Towers, follow the Gregory Developmental Road north-west for 262km to the Lynd Junction.
- From Mount Garnet, travel 162km south on the Kennedy Highway to the Lynd Junction.

The national park is a further 95km south of the Lynd Junction on the Kennedy Developmental Road. The Kennedy Developmental Road is unsealed in places and may be temporarily closed or inaccessible after heavy rain.

All roads within the national park are unsealed (dirt). Emu Swamp camping area is accessible to 4WD vehicles only, Moonstone camping area is accessible to 2WD vehicles driven with caution. The unsealed (dirt) roads become boggy, over-grown and inaccessible during the wet season (usually November to March). Don’t drive into the national park during or after heavy rain—you could be stranded for days. [If it’s flooded, forget it](https://www.qfes.qld.gov.au/prepare/flooding).

Before your trip remember to:

- Check [Queensland Traffic](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) and the [Flinders Shire Council](https://www.flinders.qld.gov.au/Waste-Water-and-Roads/Roads) for current road conditions.
- Check [Park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Blackbraes National Park for the latest information on access, closures and conditions.
- Check [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/) for current weather and warnings.

### Wheelchair accessibility

Wheelchair accessible facilities are not available in the national park.

## Staying safe

The national park is isolated, and rangers may not be on park to help you. Unexpected rain and potential vehicle breakdowns require that you plan well ahead for your visit—you must be totally self-sufficient and prepared for all emergencies.

Be sure to read [essentials to bring](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/blackbraes/visiting-safely#essentials_to_bring) and follow the guidelines below to help make your visit a safe and enjoyable one.

### Before you leave

- **Plan for emergencies.** Carry adequate food, drinking water, first-aid equipment, fuel and basic vehicle repair equipment and recovery gear in case of unexpected delays or breakdown.
- **Pack at least one form of communication equipment**. Satellite phones and [Personal Locator Beacons(external link)](https://beacons.amsa.gov.au/about/beacon-types.asp) (PLBs) are the most effective in this area. Mobile phone coverage is unreliable.
- **Leave an itinerary** with a friend or relative. Include travel routes and/or check-in points.
- **[Check current weather warning for Queensland(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/)**. Heavy rain can flow down watercourses and affect access to the national park, even if there has been no local rain .
- **Pack for hot and cold conditions** (plus flies, midges and mozzies!). Outback Queensland can be very hot during the day, and very cold overnight. Check with the [Australian Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/) for more information on average temperatures.
- **Seek local advice.** Contact relevant tourist information centres to help plan your trip.
- **Book your camp site.** To camp in a national park, you must plan well ahead and obtain a camping permit and pay your camping fee before you arrive. Remember, mobile phone coverage is very limited in Outback Queensland national parks. Visit [Camping bookings](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) for detailed information on camping bookings and fees.

### While visiting

- **Carry and drink plenty of water** and try to recreate in the cooler part of the day. Ensure you have at least one form of communication equipment.
- **Wear protective clothing.** Put on a hat, sunscreen, insect repellent and wear sturdy enclosed footwear.
- **Always carry a torch at night.** This is when many snakes are active. Remember, all wildlife is protected. Set up your camp well before dark.
- **Keep to designated roads.** Watch out for animals on the roads, they can appear at any time of day though are generally more active at dusk and dawn.
- **Drive to the road conditions.** The unsealed (dirt) road in the national park maybe rough. Visitors must be experienced in [four-wheel-driving](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/four-wheel-drive-with-care) and travel with the necessary recovery gear. During and after heavy rain the unsealed (dirt) road becomes boggy and undriveable.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

### In an emergency

**For all emergencies call Triple Zero (000).**

Mobile phone coverage is not available in the national park.

We highly recommend you visit the [Triple Zero(external link)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/) website before visiting a national park or forest. Download the [Triple Zero emergency(external link)](https://www.emergencyplus.com.au/) app to help identify your location. **Important:** if there is no mobile coverage on any network, you will not be able to reach the Emergency Call Service via a mobile phone.

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

To enjoy your time in Blackbraes National Park remember to bring:

- drinking water, sufficient for your stay
- a fuel or gas stove for cooking
- a first-aid kit, medical supplies and communication equipment
- food storage containers
- sunscreen, at hat, sunglasses and insect repellent
- bags to take your rubbish with you
- a shovel to bury toilet waste.

### Opening hours

Blackbraes National Park is open 24 hours a day. The camping areas may be closed during the wet season, (usually November to March), when wet conditions make areas inaccessible. The national park may be closed at other times due to management activities or severe weather.

### Permits and fees

Camping permits are required and [fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply. A camping tag or your booking details must be displayed at your camp site.

- [Book your camp site online](https://www.qld.gov.au/camping).
- If you cannot book online, see [camping bookings](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) for other options.

Fossicking in Moonstone Hill Resources Reserve requires a [licence](https://www.qld.gov.au/recreation/activities/areas-facilities/fossicking/north-qld/moonstone-hill).

### Other permits and fees

Various activities in Queensland parks and forests require a permit. Activities include commercial tours, social events, organised group visits, school excursions, scientific research, professional photography and the sale of photographs or vision of the park. See [Permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information **.**

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted in Blackbraes National Park or the resources reserves.

### Climate and weather

In summer, daytime temperatures can exceed 35°C. The cooler months of the year—May to August—are the best times to visit. Night-time temperatures can drop below 6°C and frosts can occur during this time. Most rain falls in the summer months—December to March—and there is little rainfall in winter. Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Hughenden (170km) and the Lynd Junction (95km). For more information, see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/blackbraes/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).
