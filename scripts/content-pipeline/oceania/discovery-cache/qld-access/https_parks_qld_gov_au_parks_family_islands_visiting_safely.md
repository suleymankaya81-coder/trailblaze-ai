## Getting there and getting around

[Family Islands National Park map(PDF, 1.4MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0030/162498/family-islands-map.pdf) Check Great Barrier Reef zoning information and maps before entering or conducting any activities in the marine parks surrounding these islands.

Visit [Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority(external link)](https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/access-and-use/zoning) for zoning information.

The Family Islands are off the North Queensland coast, between Tully Heads and Mission Beach. Dunk Island, 4.5km east of Mission Beach, is the largest and most northerly of the group. Access to Dunk Island is by either private vessel or commercial ferry transfers and day trips from Clump Point in Mission Beach or Cairns. Most of the island is protected as national park and the remainder of the island is private freehold land Council esplanade.

Wheeler (Toolgbar), Coombe, Smith (Kurrumbah), Bowden (Budg-Joo), Hudson (Coolah), Mung Um Gnackum, Kumboola, Woln-Garin and Mound (Purtaboi) islands are also part of the Family Islands National Park. These islands are accessible by sea kayak, private boat or charter vessel from Mission Beach.

[Access to Mound (Purtaboi) Island is prohibited(PDF, 235KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/162091/purtaboi-island-raa.pdf) during the seabird breeding season from 1 October to 31 March.

For details of transport operators to the islands, see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/family-islands/about#tourism_information_links).

Some islands in the group are not national park. Thorpe (Timana) and Richards (Bedarra) islands are privately-owned.

Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Wheelchair accessibility

There are no wheelchair-accessible facilities in the Family Islands National Park.

## Staying safe

To enjoy a safe visit to the Family Islands, please:

- Take care on loose, slippery and uneven walking track surfaces, particularly in wet conditions.
- For your safety, walk in daylight hours only.
- Always carry water, wear hats and sturdy footwear and try to walk in the cooler part of the day.
- As you walk, rest often in the shade as heat exhaustion can affect all walkers.
- Access to the resort's airstrip is strictly prohibited—do not enter this area.
- The resort area, including buildings, are private property and access is not allowed due to safety concerns associated with Cyclone Yasi.
- Wear sunscreen and cover up when you are swimming or snorkelling.
- Be aware of wind, current direction and tides.
- Dangerous stinging jellyfish ('marine stingers') may be present in the coastal waters at any time, but occur more frequently in the warmer months. A full body lycra suit or equivalent can provide a good measure of protection against stinging jellyfish and sunburn. Visit [marine stingers(external link)](https://beachsafe.org.au/surf-safety) for the latest safety information and first aid.
- Be aware that crocodiles can turn up anywhere in croc country, including tidal reaches of rivers, along beaches, on offshore islands and cays in the Great Barrier Reef and Torres Strait, and in freshwater lagoons, rivers, and swamps. Crocodiles are dangerous and attacks can be fatal.
- Know your own health limitations for safe snorkelling—do not put yourself and others at risk and always snorkel with a buddy so that help is at hand.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

### Essentials to bring

Preparation is the key to a safe and enjoyable visit to Family Islands National Park. Make sure you bring:

- protective clothing, sunscreen, hat and sunglasses to protect yourself from the sun
- suitable shoes for walking on rough surfaces
- insect repellent to avoid mosquito and sandfly bites
- sufficient drinking water.

### Opening hours

Family Islands National Park is open 24 hours a day, all year round except for the [seabird closure on Mound (Purtaboi) Island between 1 October and 31 March annually(PDF, 235KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/162091/purtaboi-island-raa.pdf).

### Permits and fees

Camping permits are required for all areas in the Family Islands National Park and [fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply. A tag with your booking number must be displayed at your camp site.

- Book your camp site for Coombe and Wheeler islands [online](https://www.qld.gov.au/camping).
- If you cannot book online, see [camping bookings](https://parks.des.qld.gov.au/experiences/over-counter-permits.html) for other options.
- Book your Dunk Island camp site through the resort at [Dunk Island(external link)](https://www.dunk-island.com/).

Special permits are required for [commercial](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/hospitality-tourism-sport/tourism/parks-recreation-forests) or [organised events](https://apps.des.qld.gov.au/forms/organised-events/) within the park. View [permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted on Family Islands National Park or on tidal lands adjacent to Family Islands National Park within the Great Barrier Reef Coast Marine Park. Tidal areas include beaches, rocks and mangroves.

### Climate and weather

Family Islands National Park has a tropical climate. In summer, the daytime temperatures average 30 °C with high humidity and rainfall. From April to September, the days are cooler and less humid and, despite the steady south-easterly trade winds, are usually the best times to visit. For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/family-islands/about#tourism_information_links).

Visitors should check weather conditions before setting out. The islands may be inaccessible to all boats if there are strong wind warnings, gales or cyclonic activity. Commercial tour companies cease operating ferry, water taxi and air services at these times. Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Mission Beach on the mainland. For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/family-islands/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
