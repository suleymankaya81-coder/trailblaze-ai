## Getting there and getting around

- [Overlanders Way parks map(PDF, 165KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/162260/overlanders-way-map.pdf)
- [Burdekin River camping area map(PDF, 1.1MB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/164044/burdekin-rv-camping-area-map.pdf)

The national park is 45km north of Charters Towers. Access to the western side of the national park and Burdekin River camping area is via the Gregory Developmental Road (Lynd Highway). From the south, turn right onto the unsealed Leichhart Road at [Fletcher Creek camping reserve(external link)](https://www.visitcharterstowers.com.au/fletcher-creek) and travel a further 3km to the national park entrance.

All access roads into the national park are unsealed (dirt), a 4WD vehicle is recommended. After minor rainfall the access road to Burdekin River camping area becomes boggy and can be impassable.

Before your trip remember to:

- Check [Queensland Traffic](https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au/) and [Charters Tower Regional Council](https://www.charterstowers.qld.gov.au/Services/Roads) for current road conditions.
- Check [Park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Dalrymple National Park for the latest information on access, closures and conditions.
- Check [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/) for current weather and warnings.

### Wheelchair accessibility

Facilities at [Fletcher Creek camping reserve(external link)](https://www.visitcharterstowers.com.au/fletcher-creek) (managed by Charters Towers Regional Council) are wheelchair-accessible.

## Staying safe

- Protect yourself from the sun. Wear sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses and a long-sleeved shirt, even on cloudy days.
- Carry and drink plenty of water. Conditions can become very hot and dry.
- Wear insect repellent, clothing and shoes for protection against stings, scratches and bites.
- Be observant for snakes all year round—never provoke snakes and detour around them where possible.
- Be careful around the water. Check the depth before entering and never jump or dive into the water. Be mindful of slippery rocks.
- Never walk alone. Tell friends and family where you are going and when you expect to return. If you change your plans inform them. Follow the [walk with care](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/walk-with-care) guidelines.
- Keep to designated vehicle tracks, and drive with caution as unsealed road conditions can change quickly.
- Watch out for animals, they can appear at any time of day though are generally more active at dusk and dawn.
- When cycling always wear the right safety gear and [cycle with care](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/mountain-bike-and-cycle-with-care).
- Be aware of cyclist and pedestrians who also use the road. [Drive](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/four-wheel-drive-with-care) and [ride](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/trail-bike-ride-with-care) with care and to conditions.
- Carry at least one form of communication equipment. Satellite phones and Personal Locator Beacons (PLBs) are the most effective in this area. Mobile phone coverage is unreliable.

For more information, please read the guidelines in [stay safe and visit with care](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

### In an emergency

**For all emergencies call Triple Zero (000).**

We highly recommend you visit the [Triple Zero(external link)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/) website before visiting a national park or forest. Download the [Triple Zero emergency app(external link)](https://www.emergencyplus.com.au/) to help identify your location. **Important:** if there is no mobile coverage on any network, you will not be able to reach the Emergency Call Service via a mobile phone.

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

- First-aid kit and reliable communications equipment.
- Drinking water, sufficient for your stay.
- Fuel or gas stove for cooking.
- Sealable containers for rubbish—bins are not provided. Please take your recyclables and rubbish with you when you leave.
- Sturdy shoes, a hat, protective clothing and sunscreen.
- Camera and binoculars for viewing wildlife.

### Opening hours

Dalrymple National Park is open 24 hours a day. The national park may be closed at times due to wet conditions, severe weather (generally November to March) or management operations. Check [park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for Dalrymple National Park for the latest information on national park access, closures and conditions.

### Permits and fees

Camping in the national park must be pre-booked. A camping permit is required and [fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/camping/bookings) apply. A camping tag or your booking details must be displayed at your campsite.

- [Book your campsite online](https://www.qld.gov.au/camping).
- If you cannot book online, see [camping bookings](https://parks.des.qld.gov.au/experiences/over-counter-permits.html) for other options.

#### Other permits and fees

Various activities in Queensland parks and forests require a permit. Activities include commercial tours, social events, organised group visits, school excursions, scientific research, professional photography and the sale of photographs or vision of the park. See [Permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information **.**

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted in Dalrymple National Park.

### Climate and weather

Days can be very hot from October to March, reaching an average 32-36 degrees Celsius. Hot summer days at times exceed 40 degrees Celsius. Wet periods occur during this time that can make access difficult to impossible. It's best to visit in the cooler months from April to September when daily temperatures average a more pleasant 28 degrees Celsius. Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](https://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available at Charters Towers (45km). For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/dalrymple/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
