## Getting there and getting around

- [Capricorn Coast National Park map(PDF, 144KB)](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/166250/capricorn-coast-park-map.pdf)

From Yeppoon, travel approximately 6km south along the Scenic Highway to the Vin E Jones Memorial Drive turnoff. Double Head and Rosslyn Head sections are very close to the Rosslyn Bay Marina. Continue south along the Scenic Highway to the signed turnoff for Bluff Point section. Cocoanut Point section is the most southerly section and accessed via Reef Street, Zilzie.

Contact [Queensland Traffic](https://www.tmr.qld.gov.au/Travel-and-transport) to find out about road conditions and the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/) for weather reports and forecasts.

### Wheelchair accessibility

Wheelchair accessible toilets are provided at Bluff Point section, assistance may be required.

## Staying safe

Capricorn Coast National Park is close to Yeppoon, however it is important you are prepared for your visit and emergencies.

- Before you leave, always inform someone responsible about where you are going and when you expect to return. Remember to notify your contact person when you return.
- Ensure you have enough daylight to complete your walk and never walk alone.
- Always carry a well-stocked first-aid kit, extra drinking water and reliable communications.
- Watch your step on cliff edges—edges can be unstable due to natural weathering. Stay away from the cliff edge and take care where and when you walk. For your safety, stay behind the barriers at lookouts. Observe danger signs and take heed of all safety advice and warnings.
- Wear appropriate clothing for the climate, sturdy shoes and a hat.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

### In an emergency

#### For all emergencies call Triple Zero (000).

We recommend you visit the [Triple Zero website(external link)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/) before visiting the national park. You can also download the free [emergency + app(external link)](https://www.emergencyplus.com.au/) before you leave home, the GPS functionality can provide critical location details to emergency services. **Important:** if there is no mobile coverage on any network, you will not be able to reach the Emergency Call Service via a mobile phone.

Mobile phone reception is available in all sections of Capricorn Coast National Park.

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

Plan ahead to ensure you have what you need to enjoy a safe and comfortable visit.

- Bring sufficient drinking water.
- Carry a well-equipped first-aid kit and reliable communication equipment.
- Pack strong garbage bags to take your rubbish with you when you leave.
- Wear appropriate clothing and sturdy footwear.
- Bring insect repellent and sunscreen.
- Remember your camera and binoculars for viewing wildlife.

### Opening hours

Capricorn Coast National Park is open all year round. Sections of the park may be closed at times due to severe weather or management operations, check [park alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the latest information on national park access, closures and conditions.

### Permits and fees

Various activities in Capricorn Coast National Park require a permit. Activities including commercial tours, organised events (including social events such as weddings, birthday parties or fitness training), school excursions, scientific research, and the sale of photographs or vision of the national park all require a permit. Weddings are not permitted at Bluff Point section. View [permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Dogs and other domestic animals are not permitted in any section of Capricorn Coast National Park.

However, at Bluff Point domestic dogs are allowed in the carpark only to access the beach via the entrance provided. Domestic animals are not permitted in the national park.

### Climate and weather

The Capricorn Coast experiences mild tropical climate. Temperatures typically vary from 14°C to 30°C, it rarely reaches above 33°C, however strong winds may be experienced along exposed headlands.

Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

### Fuel and supplies

The closest fuel and supplies are available at Rosslyn Bay, Yeppoon and Emu Park. For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/capricorn-coast/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
