### Upper (tableland) section

From Cairns, travel north along the Captain Cook Highway. Turn onto the Kennedy Highway, which winds up the coastal mountain range, and take the turn-off to Kuranda. Alternatively take the [scenic railway(external link)](https://www.ksr.com.au/Pages/Default.aspx) that runs from Cairns to Kuranda or the [Skyrail Rainforest Cableway(external link)](http://www.skyrail.com.au/) from Smithfield, north of Cairns.

From Kuranda, drive 3.5km along Barron Falls Road, following the signposts to the Barron Falls car park. This is the access to the [Din Din Barron Falls lookout](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/journeys/din-din-barron-falls-lookout-track-near-kuranda), which is also a stop on the scenic railway line. Wrights lookout (and the start of [McDonald track](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/journeys/mcdonald-track-near-kuranda) to Surprise Creek) is a further 1.4km along the road from the car park.

The upper section of the park can also be accessed from [Speewah Conservation Park](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/speewah/visiting-safely#getting_there_and_getting_around), a starting point for [long-distance walks](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/journeys/walking-tracks-summary#walks). From Kuranda, travel a further 6.5km along the Kennedy Highway, turn left into Speewah Road and follow the signs to Speewah Conservation Park for 5.2km.

### Lower (coastal) section

From Cairns, drive 16km north along the Captain Cook Highway and turn left onto Cairns Western Arterial (Kamerunga Road) at the roundabout.

To access Stoney Creek, from the roundabout on the Captain Cook Highway drive 3.5km along the Cairns Western Arterial (Kamerunga Road) and turn right onto Stoney Creek Road. The Smith track trailhead is on the left, 1km from the turn-off. Follow the road for 2.1km through the residential area to the car park and the start of the [Stoney Creek Garndal Garndal weir track](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/journeys/stoney-creek-garndal-garndal-weir-track-near-kamerunga) and the [Douglas track](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/journeys/douglas-track-from-kamerunga-to-speewah) trailhead.

### Wheelchair accessibility

Din Din Barron Falls boardwalk, lookouts and toilets are suitable for wheelchair access with assistance. The [Skyrail Rainforest Cableway(external link)](http://www.skyrail.com.au/) has wheelchair-accessible facilities.

## Staying safe

Take some simple precautions to ensure a safe, enjoyable visit:

- Keep on the walking tracks and boardwalks at all times.
- Always carry water, wear hats, sunscreen and sturdy footwear.
- Stay clear of the edge of the gorge and steep rock faces, and take care on uneven, slippery track surfaces, especially when wet.
- Be aware that stinging trees are found alongside many walking tracks. They grow to 4m high and have large, heart-shaped leaves with serrated edges. Do not touch this plant as it may result in a very painful sting.
- Cassowaries are potentially dangerous. Avoid unnecessary risks and help protect cassowaries. [Be CassoWARY](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/conservation/threatened-species/featured-projects/southern-cassowary#be_cass_o_wary)
- Poison fire coral fungus is known to be found in the Stoney Creek catchment. **Do not touch this fungus** as it can be extremely dangerous to touch and potentially lethal if ingested.

#### Be Crocwise

Estuarine crocodiles live in waterways of the lower section of the Barron Gorge National Park. Estuarine crocodiles are potentially dangerous. Never take unnecessary risks in crocodile habitat. You are responsible for your own safety—always [Be Crocwise](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/becrocwise) in croc country.

For more information, please read the guidelines on [safety in parks and forests](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care).

## Before you visit

### Essentials to bring

To ensure a safe and enjoyable visit always bring:

- a hat, sunscreen, sunglasses and insect repellent
- sturdy, reliable footwear
- drinking water
- rubbish bags.

### Opening hours

Barron Gorge National Park is open 24 hours a day.

### Permits and fees

Permits are required for [commercial](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/hospitality-tourism-sport/tourism/starting-up/regulations/parks-recreation-forests) or [organised](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/parks/permits/organised-events) activities. View [permits and fees](https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/permits-and-fees) for further information.

### Pets

Domestic animals are not permitted in Barron Gorge National Park.

### Climate and weather

Barron Gorge National Park has a tropical climate. In summer, maximum temperatures range from 27°C to 33°C with high humidity. Between December and April, there are frequent heavy downpours of rain and the possibility of thunderstorms and tropical cyclones.

Although you can visit the park all year round, the cooler, drier months of the year (from May to September) are the best times to visit. The weather is warm with reduced humidity and average maximum temperatures of 26°C.

Weather forecasts are available from the [Bureau of Meteorology(external link)](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/about#tourism_information_links).

### Fuel and supplies

Fuel and supplies are available nearby at Kuranda, Smithfield, Cairns and Mareeba. For more information see the [tourism information links](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/barron-gorge/about#tourism_information_links).

## Our booking system is currently unavailable

We have temporarily deactivated our Queensland National Parks booking system following a cyber security incident involving our third-party booking provider, Bookeasy.

**Queensland's national parks, state forests and recreation areas remain open.**

While the booking system is unavailable:

- **Camping:** You can camp without a camping permit.
- **Vehicle access:** You can access K'gari, Cooloola and Bribie Island recreation areas without a vehicle access permit.
- **Visitor centres:** David Fleay Wildlife Park, Mon Repos Turtle Centre and Walkabout Creek Discovery Centre remain open.
- **Tours and experiences:** Existing bookings, including Mon Repos Turtle Tours, will go ahead. New bookings can be made on the day of the tour, in person, pending capacity.
- **Existing bookings:** All existing bookings and permits remain valid, however we cannot make changes or cancellations.
- **Permits**: Visitors will not be penalised for not holding a camping or vehicle access permit while the temporary arrangements are in place.
- **Future bookings:** Please refer to our website for any updates.

Check [Park Alerts](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts) for the open/closed status of individual parks before travelling.

For booking enquiries, please email [QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au](mailto:QPWS@detsi.qld.gov.au).

**All other normal rules apply when visiting our National Parks.**
