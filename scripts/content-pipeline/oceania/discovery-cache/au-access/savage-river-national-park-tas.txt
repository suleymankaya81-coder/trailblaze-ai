This inaccessible reserve protects one of the few remaining temperate rainforests surviving in Australia and is a refuge for primitive flora, undisturbed rivers and high quality wilderness. The pristine rainforests in Tasmania are some of the only remaining cool climate rainforests that once were widespread on the supercontinent Gondwana. Savage River National Park is a place explored by few, with deep ravines and river gorges flanked by steep mountain ranges, blanket bog peat soils and isolated buttongrass moorlands. The lack of roads and facilities are in keeping with the Park’s wilderness character and have allowed this special place to continue to flourish. You can catch a glimpse of the unique rainforest ecosystems of this area by exploring some of the surrounding regional reserves. Savage River National Park is one of the few remaining temperate wilderness areas left on Earth. For more information on our wildlife please see Tasmania's wildlife . Visit our caring for wildlife page for more information on interacting with and protecting our native animals. Cultural heritage Show more/less The park lies within the Tasmanian Aboriginal Big River and North nations at the time of European contact.  The Big River people were an inland group who largely occupied the mountainous plateau of the Central Highlands.  The Northern nation occupied the central north of Tasmania from the coast south to the base of the Western Tiers. It is likely that the two nations would have visited one another’s country travelling by well-defined routes.  Many of these routes were later used by European explorers. Caves and rock shelters have been recorded in this densely forested area. It has been suggested that the area may have formed part of an extensive source of chert for the manufacture of stone tools. All Aboriginal sites are protected under Aboriginal Heritage Act 1975 .​ Aboriginal sites and the cultural landscapes of Tasmania have a strong and continuing significance to the Tasmanian Aboriginal community.​ Getting there Show more/less Buy a map from TasMap ​Savage River National Park is remote and inaccessible.​ Limited access into the adjacent regional reserve by 4WD is possible from the east via the Murchison Highway and from the south via the Waratah-Corinna Road. However, many of these tracks, particularly those from the south, have become very overgrown. The upkeep of roads and tracks on State forest to the east of the regional reserve is the responsibility of Sustainable Timber Tasmania (STT) who maintain these roads on an as-needed basis. Where possible, please avoid driving in our reserves at night. You are sharing the roads with our native wildlife, so take it slow and watch out for animals on the road.
